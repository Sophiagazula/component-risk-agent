Component shortage risk and buy plan

Device forecasts are exploded through the bill of materials and simulated 3,000 times, including late supplier deliveries. Each part's buy quantity is set by the cost of being short versus the cost of holding too much.

Agent actions with tiered autonomy

The agent acts alone only when an action is free and reversible. Spend goes to a planner up to a limit, and anything above it, or anything strategic, goes to a director. Every step is logged.

How it works

A one-day prototype on synthetic data. The point is the shape of the system: every step turns the previous step's output into a decision with dollars attached.

Forecast model

    Decision logic

    • Buy quantity: buy up to the critical-ratio quantile of lead-time demand, CR = Cu / (Cu + Co).
    • Cost of being short (Cu): share of short orders lost × device margin per part, plus expedite premium on the rest.
    • Cost of holding (Co): unit cost × (holding rate × protection months / 12 + obsolescence).
    • Shortage allocation: do nothing = fair share by demand; reallocate = highest-margin devices first.
    • Option value: margin on devices that would not ship by April, plus a late-shipment cost (default 15% of margin) on devices shipped late, minus mitigation cost.

    What I'd change with real data

    • Replace synthetic history with ERP orders and shipments (e.g. NetSuite), BOMs from the PLM system and supplier dates from planning tools.
    • Use CRM pipeline and customer rollout schedules as the leading signal, and installed-base telemetry for replacement demand.
    • Learn supplier lateness per supplier and lane from receipt history instead of a single on-time rate.
    • Validate against numbers Finance already trusts before any recommendation reaches a planner.

    Known limitations

    • All data is synthetic. Product, part and supplier names are generic.
    • The environmental sensor is intermittent and the model loses to seasonal naive there. In production I'd forecast it with a Croston-style method or at a higher level.
    • Finished-goods stock, multi-site allocation and supplier capacity limits are left out.
    • The agent briefing is generated from templates so every number is traceable. In production, an LLM would draft the wording from the same numbers.
